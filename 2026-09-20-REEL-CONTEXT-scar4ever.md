## What you're writing

A short-form vertical video script for Instagram Reels. ThinkFirst Studios is a small web design studio (founder: Alex). This reel is process content for designers, founders and small-studio owners.

Deliver: on-screen text, voiceover/caption lines, rough shot notes, a caption, and hashtags. Mostly screen recordings — no face-to-camera unless you flag it as an option.

---

## 🔴 Scope — the single most important constraint

**This reel is about the spec mockup and its design. It is not about a launched website.**

The real site isn't live. It's sitting on a `noindex` preview host with an open admin bug, and her domain still points at Squarespace. So:

- ✅ Say: built, designed, mocked up, "what her site could look like"
- ⛔ Never say: launched, live, shipped, delivered, "her new site is up"
- ⛔ No URL on screen. The preview is not for public traffic.
- ⛔ Nothing about the CMS, Sanity, the admin panel, Netlify, or deploy plumbing. Out of scope and not finished.

If the script needs a forward-looking line, "launching soon" is the ceiling.

---

## 🔴 Second constraint — do not tear down her old site

The research behind winning this job included a blunt audit of her existing Squarespace site: page weight, portfolio pages with no readable text, a homepage that argued with her own bio. **All of that was internal pitch material and it stays internal.**

She is a client now, and she is a *graphic designer*. Publicly itemizing what was wrong with her old site, with her named, humiliates her professionally. That's the fastest way to lose her and to look like a studio nobody should hire.

**The frame is additive, never corrective.** Not "her site was bad, we fixed it." Instead: "she has one of the strongest visual identities we've seen, and we built her a site that finally looks like her work."

If a before/after is used at all, the "before" is a generic template, not her actual old homepage.

## The story — designer × builder

**The hook worth leading on:** we built a website for someone who builds websites.

That's a real tension and it's rare. Most studios pitch clients who can't evaluate the work. She can. She sells logos at $550 and identity kits into four figures, and web design is a line item on her own services page. She could see every decision.

**The division of labour, which is the actual point of the reel:**

- **She owns the look.** The visual language is hers — it already existed across hundreds of posts, her own artwork, her wordmark. Nothing about the aesthetic was invented by us.
- **We made it a website.** The job wasn't to design her a brand. It was to take a brand that only lived in flat squares on Instagram and make it work as a responsive, accessible, fast thing that holds up on a phone at 375px and a monitor at 1600px.

That's the whole reel: *the designer brings the world, the builder makes it walk.*

**Her own line, from her old site, is the perfect closer** — she wrote it, not us:

> "INSTAGRAM ISN'T FOREVER BUT THIS WEBSITE WILL BE"

Use it. Credit it to her.

## Design specifics — verified off the actual build

These are the real values. Good for on-screen text and for the colour-swatch shot.

**Ground:** true black `#000000`

| Role | Hex |
|---|---|
| Pink (the brand) | `#ff0087` |
| Deep pink | `#a30057` |
| Rose | `#feabda` |
| Red | `#ff1f1f` |
| Chrome | `#c9ccd1` |
| White | `#ffffff` |

**Sections built:** hero · statement · work · services · follow · about · links · contact

**Techniques worth showing:** halftone dot screens, spray grain, print misregistration, a scrolling ticker strip, type that crops off the edge of the frame, pink-on-black at full saturation. All done in CSS — blend modes and background patterns, not exported images.

**The detail designers will appreciate:** full-saturation pink on true black clears the 4.5:1 contrast threshold, so the loudest possible version of her palette is also the accessible one. No muting required. That's a satisfying 3-second beat.

**Optional, handle with care:** the mockup homepage is ~20KB of HTML. Only use a weight comparison if it's framed as "what custom buys you," never as a shot at her old site. Honestly, it's safer to skip.

## Tone

Matter-of-fact and a little bit admiring of her work — that admiration is real and it should come through, because the reel is partly a compliment to a designer who let us build for her.

No hustle voice. No "most agencies get this wrong." No rhetorical-question stacking. Short sentences, concrete nouns. If a line could appear in any agency's reel, cut it.

## Format

- **Length:** 20–35s. Give a tight 25s cut and note which beats drop for a 15s version.
- **First 3 seconds:** the tension, immediately — *we built a website for someone who builds websites.* Or the pink hitting black. Not a warm-up.
- **On-screen text:** assume mute. Every essential claim readable.
- **Ending:** no hard CTA. Her line as the closer is stronger than anything we'd write.
- **Caption:** 2–4 sentences, same rules. Tag **@scarmosttdope**. 5–8 non-generic hashtags.
- **Music:** her register is loud and street — whatever's used should match her, not a lo-fi process-video default.

## What success looks like

Two audiences, both should land:

1. **Designers** watch it and think *they understood her work and didn't flatten it.*

She has 3,254 followers who are exactly the audience TFS wants. Getting this right is worth more than getting it out fast.
